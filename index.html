Ardú lifts Gaza’s young people over everything between them and their ambition.

Ardú is a youth initiative rising from under the rubble of the home in Gaza City where its founder’s road began. The road from ability to income here is full of things that trip a young person: nobody ahead to guide them, skills the market cannot see, doors that stay shut, power and internet that cut mid-sentence. Ardú lifts them over each one, with guidance, learning, openings and a place where the work gets done, so they can learn, earn and compete from where they are. Ardú is Irish for a lifting. Not relief. A lifting.

1,000young people a day, at the full build

A thousand young people a day, lifted.

That is the full size of Ardú: five hundred seats in the hall under the rubble where its founder’s road began, two shifts a day, and a thousand young people walking in with ability and walking out with a client, a certificate, an interview, an income. Mentors who have walked the road. Courses with partner organisations, in cycles that never stop. Power that never cuts, shared with the street after hours. And when the hall is full, the same programme online, beyond the walls.

Nobody gets there in one step. Ardú gets there in four.

The road there has four stages.

Each one is a thing you can see, photograph and count, finished before the next begins, with a receipt for everything. The first is open now.

  1. Clear

    The rubble comes off the hall beneath the home. The first photographs come from here, and the first proof that this is real.

  2. Light

    Power, water and a fibre line that holds. The day the lights come on, a ruin becomes a place.

  3. Open

    A hundred seats, two shifts, the first partner courses, and the first two hundred young people a day. The day the first certificate is issued.

  4. Grow

    Two hundred seats on the sun’s own power, a second floor towards five hundred, then online beyond the walls. The day the place starts paying part of its own way.

Everything the road to an ambition needs, in one place.

Gaza’s young people are not short of ability or ambition. They are short of the road: someone who has walked it to guide them, the skills and tools the market asks for, a door that opens, and a place where the work can be done. Ardú lifts them over each of those gaps, and measures itself by one result: young people earning, working and competing from where they are.

Guidance from people who have walked the road

Mentors and introductions from people who have done it: how to land the first client, pass the interview, price the work.

Learning that counts

Courses with partner organisations in repeating cycles, with certificates from the partner.

Openings that lead to income

Freelancer desks, remote-work pipelines and introductions, so a skill becomes a client, a certificate, an interview, an income.

A place where the work gets done

Power that stays on, internet that holds, a seat and quiet, so nothing on the road is lost to a blackout.

What we do

What changes when the road opens.

The measure is not seats filled. It is income earned and work found, published every month.

  • A student whose university now exists only online sits her lectures and her exams on a connection that holds.

  • A self-taught developer stops walking for signal and starts delivering for clients.

  • A graduate with a skill and no opening leaves with a certificate, a portfolio and an interview.

Our impact
×2A kilowatt that works twice: the hall by day, the street after hours.

Designed to carry its own weight.

Every kilowatt of solar Ardú installs works twice: ten seats by day, and outside the hall’s hours surplus power and internet for the neighbourhood at below the market price, with the income going back into keeping the doors open. Together with the freelancer desks, the place is designed to earn a growing share of its own running costs, so that every gift goes further and lasts longer.

Every pound, accounted for.

Gifts are held in the United Kingdom by an academic trustee under a signed agreement, released stage by stage to an account used only for Ardú, and spent against written quotes. Every expense gets a number, a receipt and a photograph of what it became, and supporters receive a monthly statement.

Founded by someone who walked this road alone.

Ahmed Abuhassan was pulled out of the rubble of this home in October 2023. He learned to code afterwards on a borrowed laptop, walking each morning to find a socket and a signal, with nobody ahead of him on the road. Ardú is the lifting he needed, built for the young people who need it now.
